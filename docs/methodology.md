# Canonical Research Methodology

The five stages define execution order, not the order in which users must read results. Return to earlier stages when a material gap appears and record why.

## Stage 1: Scope Definition & Parameter Alignment

Purpose: Establish the exact question, intended outcome, audience, and boundaries.

1. Preserve the original request.
2. Record goals, category tags, requirements, constraints, and requested order.
3. Keep interpretations and subquestions separate from the original question.
4. Define source-selection criteria and completion criteria.

Output: Scope brief and acceptance criteria.

Completion: Relevant evidence and a sufficiently complete answer can be identified.

## Stage 2: Multi-Source Information Gathering

Purpose: Collect credible, relevant evidence with a traceable origin.

1. Select appropriate tools and sources.
2. Search concepts tied to the scope and record queries and dates.
3. Inspect sources and capture relevant passages, dates, and versions.
4. Distinguish independent evidence from repeated reporting.
5. Extract findings and record coverage gaps.

Output: Search record, source register, and evidence matrix.

Completion: Core questions have sufficient coverage or documented gaps.

## Stage 3: AI-Assisted Synthesis & Cross-Verification

Purpose: Organize evidence and verify material claims.

1. Group findings by question, category, and practical relevance.
2. Check material AI-generated claims against underlying sources.
3. Separate findings from assumptions and interpretations.
4. Investigate conflicts in definitions, dates, methods, versions, or conditions.
5. Seek further evidence for consequential unresolved claims.
6. Preserve unresolved disagreement instead of forcing consensus.

Output: Claim-to-source map, verified synthesis, and discrepancy log.

Completion: Material claims are supported, qualified, or excluded.

## Stage 4: Goal-Aligned Technical Structuring

Purpose: Translate findings into understandable approaches and usable instructions.

1. Lead with the plain-language practical answer.
2. Provide a few supported approaches where meaningful alternatives exist.
3. Explain applicability, prerequisites, and dependencies.
4. Give ordered steps, expected results, and verification instructions.
5. Put essential qualifications beside affected actions.
6. Move optional technical depth after the usable result.

Output: Direct answer, approach comparison, and actionable guidance.

Completion: The intended audience can select and begin a suitable path.

## Stage 5: Quality Assurance & Formatting

Purpose: Check fidelity, evidence, usability, and presentation.

1. Compare the delivery with the exact question and project requirements.
2. Check category relevance and support for material claims.
3. Review prerequisites, dependencies, and instruction order.
4. Preserve the user's requested presentation order.
5. Remove unrelated opinions and unsupported certainty.
6. Confirm accessibility, export integrity, and visible limitations.
7. Record review status and unmet criteria.

Output: Finished report and completed QA checklist.

Completion: Acceptance criteria are met or remaining limitations are explicit.

## Approach A: Structured Literature & Data Synthesis

1. Define queries and inclusion and exclusion criteria.
2. Search selected sources and record search dates.
3. Screen sources for relevance and quality.
4. Extract findings into a common matrix.
5. Compare methods, definitions, dates, and applicability.
6. Synthesize around the exact question and disclose evidence gaps.

Do not label work a formal systematic review unless the applicable review standard is specified and satisfied.

## Approach B: Iterative AI-Assisted Inquiry

1. Start with a bounded inquiry aligned with the objective.
2. Identify terminology, subquestions, and candidate sources.
3. Refine queries around material gaps.
4. Inspect underlying sources and verify material statements.
5. Revise the synthesis when evidence changes it.
6. Stop when acceptance criteria are met or remaining gaps are documented.

AI output is a working input, not independent evidence.

## Tool Use and Stopping Rules

Use tools to improve evidence or execution. Do not claim an unperformed check occurred. Obtain authorization before external changes.

Stop when the acceptance criteria are met; do not add research solely to enlarge the report. If necessary evidence is unavailable, identify what is unknown, the affected conclusion, and what would resolve it.

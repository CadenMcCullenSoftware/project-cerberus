# Axiom Hive: Draft Proposal Summary and Adoption Requirements

## Status and scope

Summary of the user-supplied proposal, Axiom Hive — Principles, XPII Governance & Evidence-Based Design, Draft v0.1, dated October 31, 2026.

This is a proposal for internal review, not ratified policy, a replacement for authoritative project requirements, or evidence that the described controls operate. Adding this summary to the repository does not ratify the proposal, confer authority, or authorize deployment.

The summary incorporates the clarifications discussed during review. Its eight themes are proposal areas, not a reproduction of the draft's separate eight internal principles and values. The underlying internal source documents were not independently verified for this summary. Raw source text and private source links are intentionally excluded.

## Key proposal themes

### 1. Human authority and dignity

Humans retain consequential decision rights. AI assists with research, comparison, analysis, modeling, and documentation. Dignity, inclusion, privacy, constructive dissent, and learning from mistakes guide the proposed approach. Silence, technical capability, and generated recommendations are not authorization.

### 2. Failure prevention as a design objective

The system would expose missing evidence, test assumptions, identify risks, compare alternatives, and preserve recovery paths. Preventing avoidable failure is an objective, not a guarantee of success. The emphasis refines earlier problem-solving language and requires review rather than being represented as unchanged historical wording.

### 3. Explicit and bounded decision rights

A proposed authority matrix addresses defense, research standards, human-authority preservation, diplomacy, product adoption, data stewardship, and charter changes. Delegations would specify scope, limits, expiry, substitutes, and escalation. Named holders and delegations remain subject to confirmation; attribution or authorship alone does not grant operational authority. Unresolved authority means no consequential execution.

### 4. Traceable research and local validation

Proposed normal adoption thresholds are at least three credible references across at least two independent originating organizations and two documented real-world implementations, with measured outcomes and limitations. Repeated publications of one originating claim count as one evidence lineage.

These are internal screening defaults, not guarantees of truth. Proposals must address counterevidence, transferability, and a bounded local pilot with predefined acceptance and stopping criteria. Where precedent or implementation evidence is unavailable, proposals remain experimental and restricted to an approved research pilot.

### 5. Comparative decision briefs

Consequential briefs should present genuinely plausible paths, normally at least two, with a baseline, deferral, or bounded pilot where meaningful. They should explain evidence, consequences, uncertainty, reversibility, and conditional assessments. Do not invent unsafe or unsupported options to satisfy a count. Modeled outcomes must remain distinct from observed facts.

### 6. Private audit trails

The proposed starting point is a centralized append-only event store linked to separately managed encrypted evidence/artifact storage. Events would capture provenance, approvals, checks, actions, outcomes, and integrity information. This wording does not establish encryption of the entire event store or implemented tamper resistance.

Records should exclude hidden model reasoning, credentials, unnecessary personal data, and unrestricted raw prompts. Hashes and signatures support integrity checks, not factual truth. A permissioned distributed ledger would require a demonstrated need for shared control across organizations.

### 7. Purpose-limited retention and access

Proposed defaults include:

- Raw working content: session-only unless persistence has an approved purpose.
- Temporary debugging payloads: up to seven days, opt-in and redacted.
- Routine operational/access events: 90 days.
- Approved decisions, evidence manifests, and release records: three years after project closure.
- Incident records: three years after incident closure, subject to applicable obligations.
- Legal holds: suspend affected deletion until authorized release.
- Backups: deletion propagation within 35 days, with appropriate deletion tombstones.

These are proposed review defaults, not legal requirements. Privacy/legal review must ratify the schedule before implementation. Access would be role-scoped; emergency access would be time-limited, justified, approved, and logged. The tension between ephemeral processing and persistent audit records requires an explicit approved resolution.

### 8. Local testing of proven blueprints

Blueprint adoption would follow objective definition, precedent and failure-case collection, evidence review, contextual-fit analysis, alternatives, consequence modeling, bounded pilot, human decision, and revalidation.

The proposed 100-point rubric covers source traceability, independent corroboration, measured outcomes, methodological quality, relevance, and failure reporting. Scores of 80–100 permit adoption review; 60–79 permit controlled-pilot review; below 60 remains research-only. The score is not a calibrated probability of success or automatic approval.

Missing authorization, unacceptable harm, unverifiable load-bearing claims, or incompatible usage rights overrides the numerical score.

## Requirements before adoption

1. Complete governance, team, research/product, and security/privacy/engineering reviews.
2. Consolidate comments and record dissent and its disposition.
3. Confirm decision-rights holders, delegations, evidence-collection owners, and unresolved implementation details.
4. Obtain formal ratification from the designated human governing authority before the proposal becomes policy.
5. Before operational use, establish applicable implementation, safeguard, pilot, rollback, and release-approval evidence.

Review comments do not authorize deployment. Policy ratification alone does not establish operational readiness. Documentation, enforced controls, and verified outcomes remain separate evidence categories.
